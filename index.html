Independent AI safety research lab

Know when AI is wrong, and why.

Language models, agents, world models and robot policies increasingly act on their own picture of the world. We study whether they know when that picture is wrong: from the inside, by reverse-engineering what they represent, and from the outside, by checking what they predict against reality.

Nullius in verba: take nobody's word for it, including the model's.

Our first question · Gnomon

Does the model know when it's wrong?

That's metacognition: a model's knowledge of its own errors and limits. It happens inside the model, so we study it with the lab's two methods, which we bring to every question we take on, for any kind of model. We publish what we find, including when the answer is no.

Method · Outside in · Referee

Check behaviour against reality

Does the model's confidence match how often it's right? The Referee checks predictions against real measurements and names the cause when they disagree. When a model can't tell it's wrong, a referee can.

Method · Inside out · Anatomist

Read what the model has learned

Do its internals encode “I might be wrong”, and does that reach its actions? We began with a NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper on whether world models learn real physics or memorise. Next: do they represent the hazards they act into?

How we work

Open by default

Code, test protocols and benchmarks are public, so anyone can rerun our numbers.

Locked tests

Results come from a held-out test, run once and logged. We publish our weak numbers too.

Inside and out

Where we can, every claim is checked twice: in the model's internals, and against reality.
